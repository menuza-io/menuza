## PART A: Station inventory apps and offline sync

### What is solid (checked, no finding)

- Dexie v6 to v7 (`apps/station/src/lib/db.ts:337`) only adds three tables
  (`stockCatalog`, `stockQueue`, `stockBlobs`) and has no `.upgrade()`. Existing
  orders, staff, shifts and clockPeople are untouched. Main is at v6
  (clockPeople), so numbering is consistent. Merge-order risk only: any other
  branch that also adds v7 collides, and Dexie requires renumbering.
- Rows are stamped with `origin` (tenantApiUrl|slug). The queue is wiped on slug
  or tenantApiUrl change (`lib/store.ts:693-698`) and rows are only ever sent to
  their own origin (`queue.ts` `claimNext`). Cross-org leakage looks closed.
  Unpair keeps the rows (same behaviour as orders); a re-pair to another
  location turns them into `wrong_location` failed rows, which are visible, not
  lost.
- Idempotency: each row id is its `clientId`; entries are keyed individually;
  `duplicates` settle rows; `{clientId}` refs are rewritten to server ids in the
  same Dexie transaction that deletes the settled row (`queue.ts:559-603`). An
  entry that was tried is never edited. Undo of an in-flight or tried waste is
  queued by `clientId`. This part is well designed and well tested
  (`queue.test.ts`, `runner.test.ts`).
- The region envelope (`apps/tenant-api/src/lib/pos-inventory-http.ts`) verifies
  device, contract header, gate, strict body, `wrong_location`, staff session,
  then the receive rule, in that order. Caller location is pinned to the
  terminal's location, so a tablet cannot touch another location's counts.
- Responses are non-strict (additive fields from a newer region are stripped);
  requests are strict.
- `ar.po`: every Stock and Kitchen-prep string is translated, including six-form
  plurals. The 17 empty `msgstr` entries are all reservations strings, not
  Stock.
- Touch targets are 56 px steppers; digits sit in `<bdi dir="ltr">`; the stepper
  is forced LTR. Fine for RTL.

- Offline for days: the queue limit is 2000 items; there is no cap on blob
  bytes. 10 invoices at up to 5 MB is 50 MB, comfortably under IndexedDB quota;
  the app already asks for persistent storage. The "Too much is waiting" message
  on a count entry means the last taps are refused with a generic error;
  acceptable.

  `createImageBitmap` fallback sends only already-small JPEG/PNG/WebP; EXIF
  rotation is handled by the browser; after five failed uploads the photo is

  invoice photo is never flagged anywhere (Part B).

  item archived mid-count is refused per entry as `item_archived` (good). A new

### B2. Missing for production (launch blockers)

Launch blockers are things that make the module wrong, dangerous to the owner's
numbers, or impossible to operate. They are ordered by how directly they hurt
trust.

1. **Station count correctness: A1 (unit switch doubles a count), A2 (cannot
   count a list while the previous count awaits approval), A3 (one tablet's Send
   kills the others' entries), A4 (the "manager can see them" promise is false
   and Clear destroys the data).** A wrong approved count corrupts stock value,
   food cost and auto-sold-out for every later week. These four are the
   first-day experience of a two-tablet kitchen.
2. **Settings UI (target food cost, weekly count day, units system,
   any-staff-can-receive).** Without it the 30% target is fixed, `count_due` can
   never fire (the weekly day cannot be set), and the Station "receive" rule is
   permanently manager-only for every restaurant, so a cashier on the line
   cannot receive the morning delivery.
3. **Operator documentation and runbook (`docs/inventory.md`)**: flags and
   rollout order, kill switches (`INVENTORY_ENABLED`), invoice bucket CORS (A6),
   `INVOICE_READER_*`, tick behaviour, what to tell support when counts
   show 409. The plan promises it; nothing exists.
4. **Photo upload path verified end to end (A6)**: bucket CORS configured per
   node, or the invoice-photo feature fails invisibly after five tries.
5. **A real two-tablet, offline-to-App integration test (A16)**, run on a
   non-developer machine, before the module is switched on for a customer. Today
   nothing exercises Station against the real region and then the App review.
6. **Seed / demo script (`seed:inventory`)**: not a customer blocker, but every
   sales demo and QA pass needs it; counts, deliveries and a week of sales are
   impossible to demo credibly without it. (Treat as a launch blocker for sales
   and QA, not for users.)

Not blockers, but make them known: head-of-line blocking (A5), version skew
(A7), zombie local counts (A8), clock handling (A9).

### B4. Suggested cuts and re-scoping

1. **Do not build, for now:** barcode scanning, lot/expiry/FIFO, WhatsApp
   Business API, allergens, item photos, second-approver workflows, valuation
   history chart. All were already "not in this plan" or low-frequency for the
   target customer. Keep them as a public "later" list so sales can answer.
2. **Cut or park the Station "dishes in waste" from the first release** if time
   is short; keep the existing "Log it as waste?" on cancelled tickets (it is
   the cheaper, higher-intent path). Do build the App's dish waste first.
3. **Do not widen Station further until A1-A5 are fixed.** More screens on a
   sync layer with these defects multiplies support load. The fix surface is
   small (region `liveKey`, Station join path, `count_closed` semantics, lane
   for photos).
4. **Simplify instead of adding:** replace per-entry "Clear these" flow with
   "reapply to the reopened count" or store late entries; replace the 24 h
   catalogue-stale concept with a visible "last updated" only. Both reduce
   tablet states the owner has to understand.
5. **Treat the invoice reader as optional infrastructure:** it needs an
   in-region model and CORS-correct storage. Ship it behind `INVOICE_READER_*`
   and a runbook entry, not as a headline feature.
6. **Reports:** build the CSV export (#3) first and the full report subjects
   second; an accountant who gets a CSV on day one removes most of the demand.
